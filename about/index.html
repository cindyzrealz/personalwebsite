Cindy Zeng

I live in many places and love many things.

I was born on a Wednesday afternoon in Shanghai, China. Sometime in July of 2017 I moved to a small town next to Seattle, Washington. Now, I go to school near Boston, a place that is becoming more and more familiar to me.

I must’ve started carving stone seals sometime in fifth grade, though it feels like a lifetime ago. It is my favorite art form. Robert Frost: “poetry is the uncommon arrangement of common language.” I feel like the same might be said of carving. For me, carving is a physical act of parsing language, of traversing through word-houses, descending with a torch into the mysterious cellars beneath and ascending the attic to gaze out the window.

I take everything I love and pass it through the medium of stone. I’ve created collections revolving around Buddhist art, around tea, around my cat, around the mythology of dragons, around the last active pictographic script in the world, around Dream of the Red Chamber, around the calendar, around the Odyssey. For that last one, I translate and distill every book of the Odyssey into one word, drawn from an epithet, a Homeric simile, an entire character… I am working on doing the same for every book of the Iliad, as well as every book in the whirlwind that is my senior English class. How to distill To The Lighthouse into one word? I do not know. Perhaps I will cheat and make two, or three, or —

I love to read. On the title page of every book I own I stamp a seal (“Cindy’s Library”) I carved to preserve in the paper my devotion for the words that follow. I have a habit of memorizing my favorite poems, Chinese or English. I hope to eventually become a repository of all these arrangements of language that have spoken some bit of myself I could not express before. I love to write and would love even more to be truly good at it sometime in life. I am a translator too.

In my free time, I play the cello and tennis, both respites from my own overactive mind. I also bake the best chocolate chip cookies in the world (humbly speaking) and star-gaze. On Friday nights, you might find me singing bad karaoke or watching a movie.

I like to shuttle myself through the world on the subway, a bike, or my own two feet. The only slow walker in a family of cursed fast walkers, I have resigned myself to always falling behind: might as well take it easy and spend some time with the flowers on the trail or a particularly worn stretch of concrete wall.

How unwieldy to tell you about everything; this website is but a sketch of my art, a sketch of me.
